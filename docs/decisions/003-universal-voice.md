# 003: Universal voice and product separation

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-06-11

## Context

The first manifesto framed a13i through its founding product and opened from "the internet was built for eyes." The founder corrected both. The internet was built as a democratic space where information could flow freely; pages and screens are interface choices, and we hold the right to develop interfaces to that information however we wish. a13i serves everyone accessibility serves, with the accessibility tradition enhanced and reborn through AI.

## Decision

1. The manifesto and all positioning material speak as the collective "we" of everyone who develops a13i technology, not as one company.
2. The web is described as a democratic information space. Interface choice is a right. Access for everyone is the founding obligation that comes with it.
3. a13i addresses all accessibility needs, using W3C WAI's own enumeration: visual, auditory, physical, speech, cognitive, and neurological, plus temporary and situational needs. The guidebook's opening volume focuses on visual impairment content, with other volumes planned.
4. The Blind Computer is named only in consortium contexts (the Consortium chapter and governance material), credited as founding implementation. This supersedes the product framing in decision 001, guardrail 3, and the 2026-06-10 manifesto.
5. Official accessibility canon (W3C WAI framing, the Berners-Lee universality principle, "essential for some, useful for all") anchors the manifesto's voice.

## Consequences

- Manifesto rewritten in the universal voice.
- README founding section trimmed; EXP-001 attribution generalized; landing copy updated.
- Future content naming the founding product outside consortium contexts fails review.
